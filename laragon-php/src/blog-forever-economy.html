Overview

Five days after launch

Just five days after the launch of World of Warcraft: Forever, Azeroth's economy is already taking shape. Thousands of players are gathering resources, leveling professions, crafting equipment, and flooding the Auction House with everything from Copper Ore to Healing Potions.

But which professions are actually making money? Which markets are already crowded? And where might opportunities exist for enterprising players?

To get an early picture, we examined 27 representative Auction House items spanning all 12 gathering, crafting, and secondary professions, recording available quantities and asking prices across raw materials, intermediate components, and finished goods.

The results suggest an economy where gathering activity is producing enormous inventories, low-level crafted goods are often selling below their material costs, and a handful of processing markets carry surprisingly large premiums.

The important distinction: this is a snapshot of supply and asking prices, not a measurement of completed sales. High inventory alone doesn't prove that a market is oversaturated, and expensive items aren't necessarily profitable.

Nevertheless, several patterns are already emerging.

Copper Ore leads the sample with more than 17,000 units available, followed closely by Light Leather with nearly 14,000.

These enormous quantities likely reflect the concentration of players progressing through early leveling zones, where Copper Veins, low-level beasts, and other starter resources are abundant.

For miners and skinners, that creates considerable competition.

A stack of 20 Copper Ore at 60 copper per unit is worth just 12 silver before auction fees. A comparable stack of 20 Light Leather, priced at 27 copper apiece, is worth only 5 silver and 40 copper.

While these materials remain useful for leveling professions, their low prices may make gathering them specifically for profit less appealing.

The situation changes at higher levels.

Iron Ore has roughly one-quarter the listed quantity of Copper Ore and commands more than three times the unit price. Dark Iron Ore is dramatically scarcer in this snapshot, with only 61 units listed at nearly 50 silver each.

That scarcity is worth watching, but it may be temporary. Five days into a fresh launch, comparatively few players may have access to higher-level gathering areas.

Early takeaway: Starter-zone materials are abundant and inexpensive. Higher-level resources may present better opportunities as the player population progresses, but limited supply should not automatically be interpreted as strong demand.

The Surprising Value of Smelting Iron

Among the markets examined, Iron Bars produced perhaps the most interesting result.

With one Iron Ore required to produce one Iron Bar, the difference is substantial.

Illustrative Iron Bar economics

  • Raw material cost: 199
  • Finished bar asking price: 599
  • Proceeds after an assumed 5% Auction House cut: approximately 569
  • Potential profit per bar: approximately 370

That's roughly a 186% return on the material cost, before auction deposits or failed sales.

For comparison, a player processing 100 Iron Ore at these prices could theoretically earn approximately 3 gold and 70 silver more than the cost of the ore.

Of course, that assumes the bars actually sell at the advertised price.

The difference could reflect genuine demand from Blacksmiths and Engineers, limited smelting supply, or simply pricing that hasn't yet adjusted to the young economy.

Whatever the explanation, the gap makes Iron Bars one of the most interesting items to monitor over the coming days.

Market signal: Promising processing premium.

Leatherworking

Cheap Materials, Even Cheaper Products

Skinning is producing some of the largest inventories in the sampled Auction House.

With 13,780 Light Leather available at just 27 copper each, low-level leather is plentiful.

Unfortunately for Leatherworkers, inexpensive materials don't automatically translate into profitable finished goods.

Consider the Light Leather Quiver.

ItemQuantityPrice
Light Leather13,78027
Light Leather Quiver8141

Using the standard recipe of four Light Leather and two Coarse Thread, the estimated cost to manufacture a quiver is approximately 128.

Yet finished quivers are listed for just 1 silver.

After auction fees, the seller would receive approximately 95 copper, representing a loss of about 33 copper per quiver, excluding deposits.

Why would players manufacture items at a loss?

The likely explanation is profession leveling.

Players crafting equipment to increase their Leatherworking skill may care more about obtaining skill points than recouping their material costs. Once crafted, the item becomes surplus inventory, and anything recovered through the Auction House reduces the cost of leveling.

The result is a market where crafted equipment can be cheaper than the materials used to make it.

Market signal: Strong evidence of uneconomical early crafting at current asking prices.

Blacksmithing

Starter Recipes Face Intense Price Pressure

Blacksmithing tells a familiar story.

Rough Sharpening Stones were listed for just 9 copper, exactly the same as the sampled price of Rough Stone, their primary crafting material.

At least 1,668 units were visible across the captured price tiers.

Manufacturing these stones from purchased materials therefore offers no gross markup, and auction fees push the transaction into negative territory.

Copper Chain Vests started at 4 silver, with multiple listings visible between four and ten silver. However, a complete inventory count and comparable Copper Bar prices weren't captured.

These observations are consistent with players manufacturing early Blacksmithing items primarily for skill progression, although actual turnover remains unknown.

Market signal: Starter consumables are priced at or below economical crafting levels.

First Aid

The Clearest Example of Below-Cost Crafting

First Aid produced one of the most dramatic price comparisons.

Wool Cloth currently costs 79 copper per unit.

Wool Bandages, which use one Wool Cloth each, are listed for just 28 copper.

Even before auction fees, that's a 51-copper difference between the raw material and finished product.

With 4,889 Wool Bandages available, the market appears to contain a substantial amount of inexpensive finished inventory.

This is a textbook example of a profession where leveling generates items that players may consider disposable.

When the purpose of crafting is to gain skill points rather than produce valuable merchandise, finished goods can become cheaper than their ingredients.

For players who simply need bandages, buying finished products may be significantly less expensive than buying cloth to manufacture them.

Market signal: Strong below-material-cost pricing and substantial inventory.

Why a Fresh Economy Behaves Differently

Forever launched on November 4, meaning this snapshot captures a market only five days into its life.

That timing is critical.

Three overlapping economic forces may explain the results.

First, players are gathering far more starter materials than established markets typically need at any one moment.

Nearly every new character begins in low-level zones. Copper Ore, Light Leather, and similar materials are encountered constantly, producing considerable supply.

Second, profession leveling generates products that aren't necessarily manufactured for profit.

Players crafting bandages, sharpening stones, armor, and other starter items may happily sell below material cost if doing so recovers even a portion of their leveling expenses.

Third, the player population hasn't fully progressed into higher-level content.

Resources and recipes that become common later may temporarily appear scarce because relatively few players can acquire them.

This creates a rapidly changing market where today's expensive resource could become tomorrow's commodity.

The launch economy is therefore better understood as a snapshot of player progression than as a mature, stable marketplace.

What Happens Next?

This analysis establishes an initial benchmark, but the next measurements will be more revealing than the first.

To determine which markets are genuinely oversaturated, we'll need to compare the same products across multiple days.

Key indicators include:

  • Available quantity: Is inventory increasing or decreasing?
  • Unit price: Are sellers undercutting one another, or are prices holding?
  • Price depth: How much inventory is available near the lowest asking price?
  • Turnover: How quickly do listings disappear or get replaced?
  • Crafting margins: Are finished products consistently worth more than their ingredients?

Importantly, disappearing listings cannot automatically be counted as sales. Auctions may expire, be canceled, or be reposted at different prices.

Even with that limitation, repeated snapshots should reveal patterns that one-time searches cannot.

A market with rising inventory and falling prices may be experiencing oversupply. A market where inventory repeatedly disappears while prices remain firm may indicate healthier demand.

Neither outcome can be established from a single observation.

Final Thoughts

An Economy Still Finding Its Footing

Five days into World of Warcraft: Forever, the Auction House is already showing some recognizable features of a new MMORPG economy.

Starter materials are abundant. Profession-leveling products are often cheap. Some finished goods sell for less than the materials required to craft them.

At the same time, potentially profitable opportunities are emerging in less obvious places.

Iron Bars carry a surprising premium over Iron Ore. Savory Deviate Delight may reward players willing to convert inexpensive fish into finished food. Tailoring bolts offer small but positive margins, even while Woolen Bags appear uneconomical to manufacture.

The central lesson is that the most profitable profession isn't necessarily the one with the rarest items or the highest prices. It's the one where demand, material costs, and competition create a sustainable margin.

For now, these results provide an early look at how Forever's economy is developing. As more players reach higher levels, unlock recipes, and enter new zones, today's shortages and surpluses may change dramatically.

We'll be watching how the market evolves.
