An auction house listing tells you what someone hopes to get. It doesn't tell you whether anyone paid it. Since version 0.1.16, the WoWCensus add-on records your own auctions as they happen: when you post, when a buyer is found, and when the gold arrives in your mailbox. Match those up and you get the two numbers that matter: what the item actually sold for, and how long it took.

You don't need any other add-on or setting for this. It works with the default auction house UI and runs on its own. Only your own auctions are logged, buyers' names are never stored, and the Companion uploads the log with your normal scans. Those sales appear in the Recent Sales section of each item's page.

In its first few days the add-on has logged 119 sales of 87 different items on WoW: Forever, and the typical item sold in under three minutes. Copper Ore at about 99 copper each sold within seconds, including a stack of 20, and Light Feathers typically went within 10 seconds. Early-game materials are in such short supply that there's almost no wait.

Stack size matters, too. Single Shadowgems and a stack of three sold within a minute, while a stack of seven, priced lower per gem, waited about 14½ hours. Raptor Eggs were the same: four sold in 12 minutes, but a stack of nine sat for about 37 hours. Buyers are looking for a few, not a pile. This is a small sample, mostly from a handful of characters, so treat it as a first look rather than a market rule. Every new seller makes it more reliable.

Want to add yours? Install the WoWCensus add-on (0.1.17 or newer), then run the WoWCensus Companion and click Turn on uploads. The Companion can install or update the add-on for you. After that, just play and sell as usual: your sales upload when you log out or /reload, with no account needed. If you already use Journalator, TradeSkillMaster or Auctionator, you can share their logs too by ticking Collect other addon data in the add-on and picking those add-ons in the Companion's Settings.
